# Templates and scenes use real primitive features

Studio templates and example scenes are what people copy, so they mustn't fake a feature with parts that are there only to be invisible: a zone band at opacity 0 so its cutoff marks draw, a needle shorter than the hub so the hub draws, zero-length ticks standing in for dots. When a template needs something the studio or the primitives can't express, add the capability. The template tests (`tests/studio/templates.test.tsx`) check every template for hidden bands and needles under their hub.

- **Marks take their own values.** The cutoffs panel's `marks.values` is a free-text list of numbers, not a fixed set of sliders, so it can hold any number of marks the way `GaugeMarks values` does. Blank means the marks stay at the zone cutoffs (the placeholder says so). `markValues` (`lib/gauge-panels.ts`) drops anything that isn't a number inside the domain, so a half-typed `-` or a value left behind by a narrower domain never reaches the gauge. `band.show` hides the zones' band on its own, for marks without a band.
- **A zone can have no paint.** A band over part of the domain, like Rev tape's band past the shift point, is a zone painted `"none"`, which SVG leaves undrawn. `None` is offered on the zone colour pickers only. Every other part has a `show` toggle, and a `none` on those would just be a second way to hide them.
- **The hub draws without a needle.** `GaugeHub` never needed one. Only the studio drew it inside the needle panel, so the needle count now goes down to 0 instead of a separate hub toggle being added. The Orrery's Sun is a hub with no needles.
- **Dots are discs.** `GaugeDots` draws `<circle>`s at fixed values, as ADR 0005's round ends are discs. A zero-length line with a round cap draws the same thing, but only as a side effect of how stroke caps work.
- **Marks can follow the reading.** The music studio's fader cap is two `GaugeMarks` at `useGauge().value`, which sits exactly under the pointer. It used to light the nearest of a `GaugeTickFill`'s stops, which needed a stop count per fader to land on the value. No thumb primitive was added: a mark at the reading already is one.
- **No reversed bar.** The gain-reduction meter's scale is gain in dB, −20 to 0 as its labels read, so it draws the gain, the reduction negated, with `GaugeArc from={0}` anchoring the fill at 0 dB. A `reverse` on `LinearGauge` would have to mirror the scale for every part that reads it, and for `GaugeControl` and `LinearGaugeInset` too, to do what the anchor already does.
